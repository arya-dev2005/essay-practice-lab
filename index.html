            How to Write Under Pressure

            A practical method for placement-style essays of 160–200 words in 10 minutes.

            The 1-2-2-1 plan

            1Introduction
            2Body points
            2Examples / explanations
            1Conclusion

            Suggested pacing: 1 minute to understand the topic, 2 minutes to plan, 6 minutes to write, 1 minute to revise.

            1. Step 1 Understand the topic

              Read the topic twice. Decide your position before you type a word. A clear, simple stance beats a clever but confusing one.

            2. Step 2 Plan quickly

              Spend about two minutes noting two main points and one example for each. This outline is your safety net when the clock feels loud.

            3. Step 3 Write the introduction

              One or two sentences: restate the issue in your own words and state your position. Do not spend more than a minute here.

            4. Step 4 Develop two body points

              One paragraph per point. Begin each with a clear topic sentence, then explain or give a short example.

            5. Step 5 Give examples / explanations

              Concrete, believable examples make arguments persuasive. They can come from everyday life, news, or your studies — they do not need statistics.

            6. Step 6 Write the conclusion

              Summarise your position in one or two sentences. Signal it with a phrase such as "In conclusion" so the reader knows you finished deliberately.

            7. Step 7 Reserve time for revision

              Keep the final minute for spelling, missing words, and repeated punctuation. Small fixes here protect the marks you already earned.

            A note on practice

            This lab never writes for you. Real placement assessments measure your ability to organise thoughts under time pressure — practice expressing your own ideas without AI tools, and review honestly afterwards.
